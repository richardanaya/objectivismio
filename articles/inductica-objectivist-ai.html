Concepts, not tokens.

Conceptron is public as an idea. Ron Pisaturo designed the volumes. Learning them, and writing with them, are not.

The announcement

Midway through Inductica’s episode “Electrons as a Pure Wave Phenomenon in an Elastic Aether,” James Ellias broke in from the physics. He said: “Can we build a machine that computes in the same way that a human being thinks? Very soon, Inductica will be starting an exciting new project. My friend and mentor Ron Pisaturo has developed a new AI data structure using the Objectivist epistemology which I think will fundamentally change the paradigm in AI. I will be presenting the basics of this idea in a new series of videos very soon.”

On May 28, 2026, the channel posted a short, “Conceptual AI!” Ellias said he had just finished shooting the first video of that series, and that it would come out that Saturday. The project is named Conceptron. The playlist is public. Ellias presents it. Pisaturo, in Ellias’s words, developed it. Those are not the same job. A later video on that playlist, “Modern AI’s Fundamental Weakness,” has since been made private. This page does not use it.

Concepts as volumes

The public account is the June 13, 2026 interview, “Breakthroughs that Made Language Models Work.” Ellias opens it this way: in a language model, each word is a vector, a point in a space of many dimensions. Pisaturo, he says, will present Conceptron, “an entirely new AI architecture where these vectors are replaced by volumes.” Those volumes “represent concepts based on their ranges of measurement.” Green, in the example, is a range of hue, saturation, and brightness. Words in a language model sit near one another by usage: bush, tree, and plant. Ellias’s line in the same conversation: “In an LLM, we don’t really have concepts next to one another in a space. We have words next to one another.”

Pisaturo’s alternative uses the claim, which he attributes to Ayn Rand, that characteristics are ranges of measurement. The structural detail he actually gives is one, and he introduces it as one thing that helps, not as the whole design. Instead of a single number in each cell of a matrix, two numbers, a low and a high. For the height of a tree, he says, the lower might be 13 feet and the upper 500. “They won’t be circles, they’ll be rectangles.” Instances sit inside as points. A broader word such as plant is a larger region that encloses tree and bush.

The concretes are the point of the shape. A concept, he says, has its character “by virtue of them being able to refer to concretes,” the way “a parent is only a parent when the parent has children.” Cut the concrete reference away and keep only a point in a hyperspace, and “you’re really using the concept just as if it were a concrete just like a percept. It loses its conceptual power.”

Ellias has used the same picture in his own telling: an apple as ranges of size, hue, and sweetness, and an Asian pear splitting off when its sweetness no longer fits. “Large squirrel” and “large whale” are not the same interval of length. Those illustrations are his, from a video the channel later made private, and from Gärdenfors, not from the patent. Gärdenfors is not a party to Conceptron. In March 2026 Pisaturo wrote that Gärdenfors “seems to have arrived independently at important similar ideas,” and that there are “important differences,” which he does not list in that post.

Built on the breakthroughs

Pisaturo does not describe the project as a refusal of language models. His framing, in the June interview, is the opposite: “I looked at these breakthroughs that the language models employed and I asked how can we employ the same breakthroughs.” He credits embeddings, “mathematizing words,” as already a step toward characteristics. He credits the parallelism of matrix math on GPUs. Without that, he says, “language models today would just be absolutely impossible,” and his design has to keep the property. He credits next-token prediction, not as a joke, but as the way a model gets auto-regression: “the input itself is also the ground truth,” so no one has to label the training text by hand. The same process, he notes, is what then generates text.

Then the cost he states in the open. Change the embedding from one number to a pair, and “the later steps don’t work anymore.” With two numbers in the cell, back-propagation and gradient descent as they stand are, in his words, a new ball game. “We’re going to have to come up with new later steps.” He does not say, in public, what those steps are.

What is still not public

Ellias names the two challenges that remain. First, some method of auto-regression that can arrive at the volumes by gradient descent. The alternative, he says, is setting the ranges for each concept by hand, “a mammoth task.” Second: “how do we generate text?” Pisaturo answers: “the answers to these questions will have to wait for later or may have to wait for never because we may never tell anyone how we do that. But we do have a plan for how to do both of those things that is not yet implemented.” Ellias: “which means they are theory.” Pisaturo agrees, and he leaves the distance open. A few months. A few decades. Part of an answer, with other people supplying the rest.

So the volume is public. The learning of the volumes is not, and neither is the generation of text. This page will not invent either one. Conceptron, on their own account, is not a trained system and not a working writer of sentences.

The December 2025 post is a different document, and it should stay that way. Pisaturo linked two chats with ChatGPT. Asked how a concept relates to the instances it subsumes, the model described Rand’s theory and concluded, “This is not an LLM. It’s closer to a cognitive robot with a rational, goal-directed epistemology.” A later chat described “a Rand-style concept engine” and a hybrid with an ordinary language model. He tells the reader to check the ideas himself. He also wrote that he had just filed a provisional patent on a proposed language system, as yet unbuilt, that draws on Rand’s theory of concepts but goes in a different direction from the system suggested in that chat. He does not publish the claims. The chat is not Conceptron.

Where the idea came from

The public motive is older than the name. Near the end of the April 2023 Inductica interview on his book, Pisaturo says he started studying artificial intelligence because years in philosophy might be making him “provincial,” and that a person who wants to study epistemology has to study that field too. Machines, he added, often cannot explain why they work, and they still fail elementary cases. In April 2025 he wrote that how best to train today’s language models was not his focus. He was working, in stealth, on a new approach to language-based AI that draws on Rand’s theory of concepts and on A Validation of Knowledge. The book, announced in 2020, is an epistemology: axioms, causality, meaning, propositions, mathematics, and induction. He draws on Rand and also departs from her, especially on the perception of entities and on probability. It is not an architecture diagram.

The same idea, in induction

“Ranges of measurement” also does work that is not AI. In the August 2026 Inductica interview on induction, Pisaturo ties Rand’s claim that characteristics are ranges to a bound on a generalization: after ten green pebbles, the probability that the eleventh is green is at least 9/11. He cites Gärdenfors on convexity, the idea that induction depends on a concept occupying a convex region. He calls the result a unit of measure for how reliable a proposition is. That is epistemology. It is not a description of Conceptron.

The test

What they say they are aiming at is more truth than guessing the next word, a concept tied to the concretes it subsumes, and, as an ambition rather than a result, the hope of doing the work on one GPU on a desk, or of using a large machine for something far beyond a language model. Hallucination, Pisaturo says, is still an open question. The standard is the one the idea sets. A concept has to be able to name the units it integrated and the measurements it omitted. A sentence has to be checkable against that. The videos that exist show the volume, and they show the debt to the language-model breakthroughs. They do not show how the volumes would be learned, or how a sentence would be written. Those are the pieces he says are not yet implemented, and that he may never tell. What has been shown can be judged. What has been withheld cannot be praised.
