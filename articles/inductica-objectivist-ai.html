Inductica

Concepts, not tokens.

James Ellias will present the series. Ron Pisaturo designed the system. The design is not public yet.

The announcement

On the Inductica episode “Electrons as a Pure Wave Phenomenon in an Elastic Aether,” James Ellias paused the physics and said a different project was coming. Inductica, he said, would soon start work in public on a new AI data structure that uses the Objectivist epistemology. He named the person who developed it: his friend and mentor, Ron Pisaturo. Ellias thinks the structure will change the paradigm in AI. He said he would present the basics in a new series of videos.

That is an announcement, not a specification. The episode does not show the data structure, the code, or a working model. Ellias runs the channel and will be the one explaining it. Pisaturo is the designer. Those two roles should not be collapsed into one.

Concepts, and tokens

A language model predicts the next piece of text from statistical patterns in earlier text. It can sound as if it knows a concept. What it has is a bet on tokens. Objectivist epistemology asks a prior question: how a mind forms a concept in the first place.

On Ayn Rand’s account, a concept integrates units and omits their particular measurements, keeping the measurements available when a context needs them. A proposition then identifies something as something. Knowledge is that chain, checked against reality, not a fluent continuation of a sentence. Pisaturo has argued for years that this structure — concepts, measurement, propositions — is what a language system would have to compute if it were going to do more than guess the next word. That argument is the reason the announcement matters. It is not a description of an unpublished machine.

What Pisaturo has published

The epistemology is in his book A Validation of Knowledge, announced in 2020: a theory of axioms, causality, meaning, propositions, mathematics, and induction. He draws heavily on Rand’s theory of concepts and also departs from it, especially on the perception of entities and on probability. The book is not an AI paper. It is the account of knowledge he says the work depends on.

In December 2025 he wrote that this epistemology, perhaps with his theory of propositions, might revolutionize language models. The post links two chats with ChatGPT. In one, the model described Rand’s theory of concepts and sketched what it would take to build a language model on that theory, calling the sketch closer to a cognitive robot with a rational epistemology than to a large language model. In a later chat it described a “Rand-style concept engine” and a hybrid of that engine with an ordinary language model. Pisaturo tells the reader to check the ideas. The chats are not a build, and he does not treat them as one.

The same post says he had just filed a provisional patent on a proposed language system. He is explicit: the system is as yet unbuilt, it draws on Rand’s theory of concepts, and it goes in a different direction from the system suggested in the ChatGPT chat. He does not publish the claims. An earlier note, in April 2025, said he was working in stealth on a new approach to language-based AI drawing on that same theory and on the book. Stealth means the design was not the thing being published.

He has also published the part of the epistemology that is not the patent. In September 2026 he posted an interview James Ellias recorded with him on causality, induction, and probability, walking through the book’s argument: existence causes consciousness of it, entities teach that causality is regular, and induction is how one measures how reliably a causal conclusion is known. James had already put a narrower interview on the Inductica channel. Those conversations are public. They are not the AI series.

What is not public

No one outside that work has been shown the data structure. There is no paper here to summarize, and this page will not invent one. A provisional patent is a filing, not a machine. A ChatGPT transcript is a sketch the designer has already distinguished from his own proposal. Until Inductica publishes the series, the honest statement is the one Ellias made: a conceptual system exists in Pisaturo’s hands, and the presentation is still ahead.

What can be said without the series is the standard the series will have to meet. If the system forms concepts, it has to say which units it integrated and which measurements it omitted. If it states a proposition, it has to be able to show the evidence. Fluency is not that showing. Prediction of tokens is not identification. That is the test. The videos will pass it or they will not.
